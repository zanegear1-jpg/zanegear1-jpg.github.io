Bond Refund NZ: Tenant Checklist to Get Your Bond Back (2026)

Moving out? Here's exactly how bond refunds work in New Zealand now that it's all online, plus a free move-out checklist.

Getting your bond back should be the easy part of moving house, yet it's where a lot of renters lose money. This guide to getting a bond refund in NZ includes a free tenant bond refund checklist for before, on and after moving day, and explains how a tenancy bond refund works now that Tenancy Services has moved bonds fully online through Bond Hub.

The short version: bond disputes are almost always about condition and money owed. The tenant with dated photos, written messages and a clean handover usually wins the argument before it starts.

How a bond refund works in NZ (Bond Hub, since 29 June 2026)

From 29 June 2026, Tenancy Services stopped accepting paper bond refund, change of tenant and change of landlord forms. Here's the process today, according to Tenancy Services:

  1. Your landlord submits the refund in Bond Hub or their property management software, including any amount they're claiming and how the bond is split between tenants.
  2. You get a text or email with a link and a one-time password. Texts come from 2910 and emails from bonds@tenancy.govt.nz.
  3. You log in with your tenant number and agree or disagree with the split.
  4. You have 12 working days to respond. Once all tenants agree, the bond is paid the following day into the bank account you enter.
  5. If you disagree, the request is cancelled and nothing is paid. The landlord needs to talk with you and submit a new request.

Keep your tenant number safe

You need your tenant number to respond to a refund. Tenancy Services warns not to share it with anyone. Check your contact details are current in Bond Hub before you move, or the notification may go to an old email address.

Before you move out: notice and timing

Get the notice right first, because rent owed is the most common bond deduction.

  • Periodic tenancy: tenants must give at least 21 days' written notice, unless the landlord agrees in writing to less (Tenancy Services).
  • Fixed-term tenancy: to stop it rolling into a periodic tenancy, notice is given between 90 and 21 days before the end date (Tenancy Services).
  • Put notice in writing (email is fine) and keep a copy.

Then book your exit inspection with the landlord or property manager, ideally a time when you can both walk through together.

Evidence: the photo method that wins bond arguments

Your move-in condition report and photos are what make the move-out conversation simple. If you didn't take good photos at the start, take them now and keep everything dated.

  • Wide shot from each corner of every room, then close-ups of marks, stains, chips and cracks.
  • Name files clearly, for example 2026-10-07_kitchen_bench-scratch.jpg.
  • Photograph meters, keys and appliances, including any existing damage.
  • Use a simple condition code for each item: OK, wear, damage, mould or moisture, repair needed, safety concern.

Fair wear and tear vs damage

Scuffs and fading from normal living are different from holes, burns or broken glass. Matching photos from move-in and move-out make that difference obvious to everyone, including the Tenancy Tribunal if it gets that far.

Healthy homes and repairs during the tenancy

All private rentals have had to comply with the healthy homes standards since 1 July 2025, unless an exemption applies. Heating, insulation, ventilation, moisture and drainage, and draught stopping are the landlord's responsibility.

Why does this matter for your bond? Mould and moisture claims often surface at move-out. If you reported a leak, a broken extractor fan or persistent mould in writing during the tenancy, that record helps show the problem wasn't caused by you. Dealing with pests? If you disagree with a bond deduction

Don't just argue over Messenger. Work through it calmly:

  1. Decline the refund request in Bond Hub if the amounts are wrong. Nothing is paid until everyone agrees.
  2. Reply in writing with your evidence: move-in photos, repair requests, receipts.
  3. Offer a partial agreement if some items are fair. It often settles things quickly.
  4. Get free help from Community Law or call Tenancy Services on 0800 836 262.
  5. Apply to the Tenancy Tribunal if you still can't agree. Keep your replies factual and unemotional.

Bond refund NZ basics: maximum bond and lodging rules

  • A general bond can be up to four weeks' rent; a pet bond up to two weeks' rent (Tenancy Services).
  • Landlords must lodge your bond within 23 working days of receiving it. You can check your bond record and contact details online.
  • With flatmates, everyone named on the bond needs to agree to the refund split, so sort that out before moving day.

Frequently asked questions

How long does a bond refund take in NZ?

Once your landlord submits the refund in Bond Hub, every tenant on the bond has 12 working days to respond. Tenancy Services says that once all tenants agree, the bond is refunded the following day. Paper and emailed refund forms were retired on 29 June 2026.

Can I apply for a bond refund online or with a form?

Paper bond refund forms were retired on 29 June 2026. Your landlord submits the refund in Bond Hub (or their property software) and you respond online using the link, your tenant number and a one-time password. If your landlord won't submit it, call Tenancy Services on 0800 836 262 and they can help you apply.

What if I disagree with the amount my landlord claimed?

Decline the request in Bond Hub. That cancels it and no money is paid out. Then talk to your landlord with your dated evidence. If you still can't agree, either party can apply to the Tenancy Tribunal.

What's the maximum bond a landlord can charge?

A general bond can be up to four weeks' rent. If you have a pet, a separate pet bond of up to two weeks' rent can also be charged. Landlords must lodge bond money with Tenancy Services within 23 working days of receiving it.

Can my landlord keep bond for normal wear and tear?

Fair wear and tear from ordinary living is generally the landlord's cost, while damage beyond that may be claimed. Your move-in photos are the best way to show what was already there. See Tenancy Services' damage pages for detail.
