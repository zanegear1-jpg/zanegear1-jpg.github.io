How to Save Money in NZ: Pay Yourself First and Automate Your Savings

Saving what's left at the end of the month rarely works. Here's how to flip it so savings happen automatically on payday.

Most of us budget backwards: pay comes in, bills go out, we spend through the month, and whatever's left (often nothing) gets saved. If you're wondering how to save money in NZ when nothing is ever left over, flip it and automate your savings. On payday, before you've had a chance to spend it, a set amount moves into savings automatically. You live on the rest.

It's called paying yourself first, and it works because it takes willpower out of the equation. You decide once, not every payday.

How to start saving money: why automation beats willpower

  • You decide once. No monthly debate about whether you can "afford" to save.
  • You don't miss what you never see. Money that leaves before you spend is less tempting.
  • Small amounts add up. A regular amount kept up for years becomes a real buffer.
  • It's flexible. Start small and raise it when you're ready.

How to automate savings in NZ: step by step

Every major New Zealand bank, including ANZ, ASB, BNZ, Kiwibank and Westpac, and most smaller banks and credit unions, lets you set up automatic payments between your own accounts in the app or online banking. Menu names differ, but the steps are the same.

  1. Find your real payday. Check when pay actually lands. It can be a day after the payslip date.
  2. Open a separate savings account, ideally with no card attached, so the money is a little harder to dip into. Compare interest, fees and withdrawal conditions. Some accounts only pay bonus interest if you don't withdraw that month.
  3. Name it. "Emergency fund" or "Future me" works better than "Savings 2".
  4. Create the automatic payment. From your everyday account, to your savings account, for your chosen amount, matching your pay frequency, starting on payday or the day after.
  5. Write it down so you remember what you set up and why.
  6. Review in three months. Comfortable? Raise it a little. Too tight? Lower it, but don't stop.

Pay varies week to week?

Set a smaller fixed amount, then top it up manually in good weeks. If a payment bounces, move the date a day later than your pay arrives.

Pick your buckets

Many people find it easier to split savings into a few purposes:

Where KiwiSaver fits

If you're in KiwiSaver through work, part of your pay already goes to future you before it reaches your account. A few current settings worth knowing (always check IRD's KiwiSaver changes page for updates):

  • From 1 April 2026, the default employee and employer contribution rate rose from 3% to 3.5%.
  • The government contribution is 25 cents for every $1 you contribute, up to $260.72 a year, which means contributing about $1,042.86 of your own money each KiwiSaver year to get the maximum. Eligibility rules apply, including an income limit.

The savings you automate yourself sit on top of KiwiSaver, and can be reached when life happens. Saving for a house? KiwiSaver may help with a deposit: see our first home buyer checklist NZ.

The "revenge save": make impulse control satisfying

When you talk yourself out of something you didn't need, move that exact amount into savings right away.

  • Skipped the $7 coffee? Move $7.
  • Didn't buy the $60 jacket on sale? Move $60.
  • Cancelled a subscription you never used? Move that amount every month.

It turns "missing out" into a visible win, and it adds up. Keep it light: planned treats are part of a budget you can stick to.

When money's tight

Some months you'll fall short. That's normal.

  • Lower the amount instead of cancelling. Even $5 keeps the habit alive.
  • Use the emergency fund for emergencies. That's what it's for.

Frequently asked questions

How much should I automatically save each pay?

There's no single right number. Some people start with 1% of take-home pay, some 10%, some a flat $20 a week. Pick an amount you can keep up without going short on rent, food, power or debt repayments, then nudge it up every few months.

Does KiwiSaver count as paying yourself first?

Yes, in a sense. Your KiwiSaver contribution comes out before your pay lands, so it's already automatic. From 1 April 2026 the default employee and employer rate rose from 3% to 3.5%. Most people still benefit from separate savings they can access, like an emergency fund.

Should I pay off debt or save first?

If you have high-interest debt such as credit cards or buy now pay later, paying it down is often part of paying yourself first. Sorted has free guidance, and MoneyTalks (0800 345 123) offers free, confidential help from financial mentors.

What is a revenge save?

When you talk yourself out of an impulse buy, move that same amount into savings straight away. Skipped the $7 coffee? Move $7. It turns 'missing out' into a visible win.

What does pay yourself first mean?

It means moving a set amount into savings or investing as soon as you're paid, before you spend on anything else, then living on what's left. Setting it up as an automatic payment on payday is what makes it stick.
