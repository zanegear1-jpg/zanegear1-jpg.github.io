First Home Buyer Checklist NZ: Deposit, KiwiSaver and Pre-Approval (2026)

Buying your first home in Aotearoa? Work through it in the right order: money, schemes, pre-approval, then offers.

Buying your first home is one of the biggest financial moves most Kiwis make, and the order you do things in matters. Think of this as a first home buyer guide for NZ in checklist form. This first home buyer checklist for NZ walks you through the money reality, the government and KiwiSaver options that actually exist in 2026, what lenders want for pre-approval, and the checks to do before you sign anything.

We won't invent grant amounts or interest rates. Schemes and bank criteria change, so we link to the official pages and show you what to ask.

Step 1: First home buyer NZ deposit and the true cost

The purchase price is the headline. Your cash need is bigger. Build a budget that includes:

Cost What it covers
Deposit Often 20% for standard lending; less via a low-deposit product
Legal and conveyancing Lawyer's fees and disbursements (get a fixed quote)
Due diligence Building inspection, LIM report from council, title check
Lender costs Application fees, lender's mortgage insurance if low deposit
Moving Truck, rent overlap, time off work
Day-one costs House insurance from settlement, locks, curtains, smoke alarms
Rate buffer A cushion if repayments or council rates rise

Step 2: First home buyer NZ requirements for KiwiSaver and First Home Loan

First Home Grant: closed. Kāinga Ora stopped taking new applications on 22 May 2024. Remove it from your plan unless the official page changes.

KiwiSaver first-home withdrawal. According to Kāinga Ora:

  • You must have been a KiwiSaver member for at least three years.
  • At least $1,000 must stay in your account.
  • You must intend to live in the property: no investment properties.
  • First home buyers apply through their KiwiSaver provider. Previous homeowners may qualify too, but need a Kāinga Ora eligibility check first.
  • Approved funds are paid to your lawyer on or before settlement.

Kāinga Ora First Home Loan. A low-deposit option through participating lenders. The May 2026 brochure lists:

  • A minimum 5% deposit.
  • Before-tax income in the last 12 months under $95,000 (individual), or $150,000 (individual with dependants, or multiple buyers combined).
  • NZ citizen, permanent resident or resident visa holder, buying a home to live in.
  • A 1.2% lender's mortgage insurance premium, plus any lender application fee.

Criteria change, so screenshot the official page with the date when you check it.

Step 4: Get pre-approval the smart way

Pre-approval (sometimes called conditional approval) tells you roughly how much a lender may lend, based on what you've told them. It's usually subject to valuation, satisfactory due diligence and final underwriting, and it expires.

Ask every lender or mortgage adviser the same questions so you can compare:

  1. What deposit do I need for this scenario: standard lending or First Home Loan?
  2. What test interest rate are you using to assess my repayments?
  3. What fees apply at approval, unconditional and settlement?
  4. How long does pre-approval last, and what would void it?
  5. If you're an adviser, how are you paid?

Step 5: Offers and due diligence

  • Conditions protect you. Common ones are finance, building report, LIM and solicitor's approval. Auctions are usually unconditional, so do your checks before auction day.
  • Commission your own building report. Seller-supplied reports are a starting point, not a substitute.
  • Unit titles and apartments: read body corporate minutes, levies and any weathertightness history.
  • Write your walk-away number before open homes. Emotion is expensive.

The Real Estate Authority's settled.govt.nz explains every stage of buying in plain English and is worth reading end to end.

Frequently asked questions

Is the First Home Grant still available in NZ?

No. Kāinga Ora stopped accepting new First Home Grant applications from 22 May 2024. Don't include a grant in your deposit plan unless the official Kāinga Ora page says it has been reinstated.

How long do I need to be in KiwiSaver to use it for a first home?

At least three years. Kāinga Ora says eligible members can withdraw their savings, but at least $1,000 must stay in the account, and you must intend to live in the home. Apply through your KiwiSaver provider.

How much deposit do I need for a first home in NZ?

For standard lending, many banks want around 20%. Low-deposit lending is possible but limited. The Kāinga Ora First Home Loan allows a 5% deposit through participating lenders if you meet the criteria: as at the May 2026 brochure, income caps are $95,000 for an individual or $150,000 for an individual with dependants or multiple buyers, and a 1.2% lender's mortgage insurance premium applies.

Can I withdraw all my KiwiSaver for a first home?

Not quite all. Kāinga Ora says eligible members can withdraw their KiwiSaver savings, including tax credits, but at least $1,000 must stay in the account. You need at least three years' membership, must intend to live in the home, and the money is paid to your lawyer for settlement.

Do I need a lawyer to buy a house?

In practice, yes. A property lawyer or licensed conveyancer reviews the sale and purchase agreement, handles title checks and settlement, and receives any KiwiSaver withdrawal on your behalf. Line one up before you start making offers.
