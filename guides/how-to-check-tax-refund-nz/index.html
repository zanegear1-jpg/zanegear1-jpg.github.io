How to Check If I Get a Tax Refund in NZ: A myIR Step-by-Step Guide

Wondering whether Inland Revenue owes you money? Here's how the end-of-year process works and exactly what to check in myIR.

Every year thousands of New Zealanders get a little surprise from Inland Revenue, while others get a bill they didn't see coming. If you're asking how to check if I get a tax refund in NZ, the good news is that you usually don't need to apply or pay anyone. Inland Revenue (IRD) works it out for most people automatically. Your job is to make sure the numbers are right and your details are current.

This guide explains the timeline, what to look at in myIR, the usual reasons people end up with a refund or a bill, and a free checklist to get organised. It deliberately doesn't list tax rates: those change, and IRD's figures are the official answer.

Tax refund NZ 2026 dates: the end-of-year timeline

New Zealand's tax year runs from 1 April to 31 March. After it ends, IRD looks at everything it knows about your income and the tax already deducted, then issues an income tax assessment.

According to IRD's end-of-year timeline:

  • Last weekend in May to the end of July: myIR users receive their assessment, or a request for more information.
  • If IRD is waiting on income details from an employer or bank, your assessment may not come until July.
  • Refunds are paid when your assessment is processed, straight into the bank account IRD holds for you. Not everyone gets theirs at the same time.
  • Tax bills are usually due by 7 February the year after you receive the bill (IRD).

Who doesn't get an automatic assessment?

If you have income that hasn't had tax taken off (for example self-employed or rental income), you'll generally need to file an individual tax return (IR3) instead. The usual IR3 due date is 7 July, unless you have a tax agent with an extension. Check IRD's IR3 page if that's you.

How do I know if I get a tax refund? Check myIR

Log in to myIR and work through these. Menu names change from time to time, so follow IRD's on-screen labels.

  1. Find your income tax assessment for the year. It shows your income, the tax you paid and the result: a credit means you paid too much (refund), a debit means you have tax to pay.
  2. Check every income source is listed. Compare against your payslips, final pay from any job you left, and bank interest.
  3. Check tax deducted from each source. Copy the figures exactly into a worksheet rather than estimating.
  4. Check your bank account details. Wrong details are a common cause of delays.
  5. Look for messages from IRD asking for more information. These have deadlines.
  6. Check your PIR if you're in KiwiSaver or another PIE fund. A wrong prescribed investor rate can mean paying too much or too little tax on investment income.

Why refunds and tax bills happen

The tax taken from your pay during the year is based on your tax code and each pay cycle. It doesn't always match your tax for the whole year. Common reasons include:

  • You changed jobs, or had more than one job, during the year.
  • You were on the wrong tax code for some of the year, including a secondary code.
  • You worked only part of the year.
  • Interest had tax taken at the wrong resident withholding tax (RWT) rate.
  • Your PIR on PIE investments, including KiwiSaver, was wrong.

Knowing the reason helps you fix it for next year, which is the real win.

Donations, Working for Families and other extras

Some things sit alongside your income tax assessment rather than inside the simple refund check.

  • Donation tax credits need receipts and have eligibility rules. Use IRD's donations pages rather than a percentage you saw online.
  • Working for Families has its own end-of-year square-up. IRD may use a refund in one area to pay a bill in another.
  • Student loans and child support debts can also be paid from a refund. IRD sends a separate letter if that happens.

Be careful with refund promises

If a website, app or social media post promises "you're owed hundreds", slow down. IRD now calculates refunds automatically for most people. Some third-party services charge a fee or take a cut for help you may not need. Got a refund coming? The 10-minute habit that prevents surprises

Whenever you start a new job, check your tax code. Whenever you join a KiwiSaver or managed fund, check your PIR. Those two minutes save a lot of end-of-year confusion.

If you think the assessment is wrong

Don't ignore it. Gather your payslips and statements, then correct the assessment in myIR or call IRD on 0800 227 774. If your situation is complicated (overseas income, a business, trusts), talk to a registered tax agent.

Frequently asked questions

When are tax refunds paid in NZ in 2026?

IRD says myIR users can expect their income tax assessment between the last weekend in May and the end of July. For the tax year that ended on 31 March 2026, that meant late May to July 2026. Refunds are paid when your assessment is processed and usually reach your bank account within a few days. People who don't use myIR get their assessment later by post.

Do I need to apply for my tax refund?

Usually not. For most people on salary, wages and some other income, IRD works out the result automatically and pays any refund into the bank account it has on file. You may even see the money before the assessment letter arrives.

Why is my refund smaller than expected, or missing?

Common reasons are an out-of-date bank account in myIR, income IRD is still waiting on, or a refund being used to pay a debt you owe IRD (such as a student loan, child support or Working for Families debt). IRD says it will tell you by letter if a refund is used this way.

Should I use a tax refund company?

That's your call, but read the terms first. Some charge fees or take a share of your refund for things you may be able to check yourself for free in myIR. If your situation is complex, a registered tax agent may be worth it.

What if my assessment is wrong?

Check it carefully against your payslips and bank interest. If something is missing or wrong, IRD says you can correct it in myIR or call them on 0800 227 774.
