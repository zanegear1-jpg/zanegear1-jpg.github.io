Emergency Grab Bag Checklist NZ: What to Pack for 72 Hours

Quakes, floods, storms, tsunami: here's what to pack, how much water you really need, and how to make a household plan in an afternoon.

Emergencies in New Zealand rarely give much warning. An emergency grab bag checklist for NZ households is the simplest bit of insurance you can have: a bag per person ready to go, plus enough supplies at home to look after yourselves for at least three days. Civil Defence calls this being "ready for 72 hours", because help can take that long to reach everyone after a big event.

This guide follows the current advice on Get Ready, New Zealand's official Civil Defence preparedness site, and turns it into a checklist you can tick off this weekend.

Why 72 hours?

After a major earthquake, flood or storm, roads can close, power and water can be cut, and emergency services focus on the most urgent cases first. Get Ready encourages every household to be able to manage at home for three days or more. If you can stretch your supplies further, even better.

Grab bag vs home supplies

  • Grab bag: a small backpack for each person, kept where you can reach it fast, for when you have to leave in a hurry.
  • Home supplies: water, food and essentials stored at home for when you have to stay put.

You need both. Many families keep the grab bags by the door or in the car and the home supplies in a crate in a cupboard.

Emergency water storage NZ: how much do you need?

Get Ready's guidance is clear: at least 3 litres of drinking water per person, per day, which is at least 9 litres per person for three days (Get Ready). For a family of four, that's 36 litres before you count pets.

Storing tap water the Get Ready way

Clean plastic soft-drink bottles with hot water (not boiling). Fill to overflowing. Add five drops of plain, unscented household bleach per litre and don't drink it for at least 30 minutes. Label with the date. Avoid milk bottles, because milk protein can't be fully cleaned out. Stored this way it keeps for up to a year.

You can also freeze water in clean ice-cream containers. They help keep the freezer cold in a power cut and can be drunk once thawed.

Make a one-page household plan

A plan matters as much as the gear. Sit down for 30 minutes and write down:

  1. Your hazards. Earthquake, flood, tsunami, landslide, volcanic ash or severe storms. Check your council's hazard maps.
  2. Where you'll meet if you're separated, and an out-of-town contact everyone can text.
  3. School and work pick-up plans, including who collects the kids if roads are closed.
  4. Pets: carriers, leads and where they'd go.
  5. Utilities: where the water toby and power mains are. Only turn off gas or power if it's safe and you know how. If you smell gas, get out and call from outside.
  6. Medical needs: anyone who relies on power, medication or mobility aids.

When the ground shakes

  • Drop, Cover and Hold during the shaking.
  • If you're near the coast and the quake is long or strong, move to high ground or inland straight away. Don't wait for an official warning. Get Ready's rule: Long or Strong, Get Gone (Get Ready tsunami advice).
  • Listen to the radio and follow official instructions. In an emergency, call 111.

Frequently asked questions

How much water should I store for an emergency in NZ?

Get Ready says to store at least 3 litres per person per day for three days: at least 9 litres per person. That's about four 2.25-litre soft-drink bottles each. Store extra for babies, pets, cooking and cleaning if you can.

What should be in a grab bag in NZ?

Get Ready suggests walking shoes, warm clothes, a raincoat and hat, water and snacks, hand sanitiser, a portable phone charger, cash, copies of important documents and photo ID, plus any medication, a first aid kit, a torch, a radio and batteries. Pack one bag per person, and remember babies and pets.

How do I store tap water safely for emergencies?

Use cleaned plastic soft-drink bottles, not milk containers. Get Ready says to add five drops of plain, unscented household bleach per litre, wait at least 30 minutes before drinking, label it with the date, and store it somewhere cool and dark.

When should I evacuate for a tsunami in NZ?

If you're near the coast and feel an earthquake that is long (more than a minute) or strong (hard to stand up), move immediately to high ground or as far inland as you can. Don't wait for an official warning: Long or Strong, Get Gone.
