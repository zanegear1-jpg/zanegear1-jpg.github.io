How to Get Rid of Bed Bugs in NZ: A Calm, Step-by-Step Plan

Waking up itchy and worried? Here's how to confirm bed bugs, stop them spreading and get the right help, without panic or random chemicals.

Finding bites in the morning and dark specks on the sheets is horrible, but bed bugs are a pest problem, not a hygiene judgement. Anyone can pick them up from travel, second-hand furniture or a neighbouring flat. If you're searching for how to get rid of bed bugs in NZ, the most important thing is to act methodically: confirm, contain, clean, then get professional help if signs persist.

This guide is household information only. It doesn't recommend specific pesticides or DIY chemical recipes.

Step 1: Bed bugs NZ signs and symptoms

According to Health New Zealand:

  • Adults are reddish-brown, flat and oval, about 4 to 7 mm long.
  • Eggs are white and around 1 mm.
  • They hide in dry, dark places: mattresses, headboards, bed frames, furniture, curtains, and cracks in walls and floors.
  • Signs include live bugs, cast-off skins, eggs, and small dark brown or black faecal marks.
  • Bites often look like mosquito or sandfly bites and may not appear for a few days. Some people don't react at all.

Look-alikes exist, including carpet beetles and fleas, so photograph anything you find before you clean it away.

Don't bomb the house

Setting off random sprays or foggers can scatter bed bugs into new rooms and create safety risks for people and pets. Get the inspection done first and follow professional advice on treatment.

Step 2: Inspect room by room

Use a torch and an old card to check seams and cracks. Start with the bed, then work outwards. Bed bugs can't fly, but they crawl short distances, so check other bedrooms too.

Step 3: Contain and clean (no chemicals needed)

Health New Zealand says you can kill bed bugs by washing and drying bedding and clothing at high temperatures. Practical steps:

  1. Hot wash and dry bedding and clothing on the hottest settings the fabric labels allow.
  2. Bag what you can't wash in sealed plastic bags until you've got professional advice.
  3. Vacuum mattress seams, bed frames, skirting and carpet edges. Empty the vacuum into a sealed bag and put it in the outside bin.
  4. Declutter around the bed so inspection and treatment are possible.
  5. Pull the bed slightly away from the wall and keep bedding off the floor.
  6. Consider a bed bug mattress encasement after cleaning, following the product instructions.

Step 4: Bed bugs NZ treatment: when to call a professional

Cleaning helps, but bed bugs often hide in furniture and walls. Health New Zealand notes that once you've found the infestation, treatment is usually needed, and you could hire a pest control expert. Call one if:

  • you've confirmed or strongly suspect an infestation
  • more than one room is involved
  • signs keep coming back after a thorough clean
  • you rent (so the landlord can arrange it promptly).

Ask what method they use, what preparation they need from you, and whether follow-up visits are included. Keep receipts.

If you rent: your rights and responsibilities

Tenancy Services says:

  • Landlords must make sure the property is free from pests at the start of a tenancy and maintain it to help prevent infestations.
  • Tenants must keep the place in a condition that doesn't encourage pests, and tell the landlord straight away if they notice signs.
  • If pests appear later and no one caused them, responsibility is unclear, and landlords and tenants often split the cost.
  • If the house needs fumigating, landlords should take responsibility.

Tell your landlord in writing, include dated photos, and coordinate treatment so everyone follows the preparation instructions. When you eventually move out, our bond refund NZ checklist helps you document everything.

Step 5: Prevent them coming back

  • Travelling: check hotel mattress seams, keep bags off the floor and bed, and hot-wash clothes when you get home. Vacuum your bags, including the creases.
  • Second-hand goods: inspect furniture outside before bringing it in. Health New Zealand advises avoiding second-hand mattresses.
  • Ongoing: re-inspect weekly for several weeks after treatment.

Bed bug bites in NZ: health advice

Most bites settle on their own. Avoid scratching, and ask a pharmacist about antihistamines for itching. If you're worried, call Healthline free on 0800 611 116. Signs of a severe allergic reaction are an emergency: call 111.

Frequently asked questions

What do bed bugs look like?

Health New Zealand describes adults as reddish-brown, flat and oval, around 4 to 7 mm long. Eggs are white and about 1 mm. You may also see cast-off skins and small dark brown or black marks on bedding or mattress seams.

Can I get rid of bed bugs myself?

Washing and drying bedding and clothes at high temperatures kills bed bugs, and vacuuming and decluttering help. But bed bugs hide in furniture and cracks, so established infestations usually need a pest control professional.

Who pays for bed bug treatment in a rental?

Tenancy Services says landlords must provide a property free of pests at the start of a tenancy. If pests appear later and no one caused them, responsibility is unclear and landlords and tenants often split the cost. Tell your landlord in writing straight away.

Are bed bug bites dangerous?

Usually not. Health New Zealand says to avoid scratching and use an antihistamine for mild reactions. Severe allergic reactions (anaphylaxis) are very rare but are an emergency: call 111. For advice, call Healthline free on 0800 611 116.

Can I get rid of bed bugs overnight?

Realistically, no. Hot washing and drying kills the bugs it reaches, but bed bugs and eggs hide in seams, furniture and cracks. Expect several weeks of cleaning, treatment and re-checking, and use a pest control professional for an established infestation.
