How an ADHD planner helps: five principles

  1. One home for open loops. If a task isn't on your list, it doesn't exist yet. One list, not five.
  2. A Monday-start week. It matches most NZ work and school weeks and many bill cycles.
  3. Tiny next actions. "Open the IRD letter" beats "sort out finances".
  4. Short, timed sprints. 10 to 25 minutes on one category, then stop.
  5. A short weekly reset. 15 to 25 minutes on purpose. Long reviews don't happen.

Try body doubling

Many people find admin easier with someone else present. Do a 25-minute admin sprint on a video call with a friend, both on mute, each doing your own thing.

Paper and email triage: the four-pile method

Set a 15-minute timer and sort everything into four piles or folders:

  • Action: needs you to do something
  • Waiting: you're waiting on someone else
  • File: keep, nothing to do
  • Recycle or shred: anything with personal details goes in the shred pile

For email: star means Action, snooze means Waiting, a label means File, and unsubscribe ruthlessly.

A bill-pay routine that forgives you

  • Automate fixed bills you trust, like rent and phone plans.
  • Keep a short manual list for variable bills like power and any IRD payments.
  • Build a small buffer in your bills account if you can. Even a little reduces the stress of a bounced payment.
  • If you can't pay in full, ask early. Many NZ utilities and lenders have hardship teams.
  • Make saving automatic too. Our pay yourself first guide uses the same set-and-forget idea.
  • IRD letters piling up? Our tax refund NZ guide walks through myIR step by step.

Frequently asked questions

Why is life admin so hard with ADHD?

Many people with ADHD describe admin as boring, unclear and easy to put off, which is a tough combination. External systems (one list, reminders, short timed sprints) can help. If it's seriously affecting your life, talk to your GP about assessment and support.

What's the best ADHD planner layout?

The one you'll actually open. Many people find a single weekly page with a 'top three' and one small admin win per day works better than a detailed daily planner that becomes another source of guilt.

What should I do if I can't pay a bill on time?

Contact the provider before the due date. Many NZ power, phone and finance companies have hardship teams and payment plans. MoneyTalks (0800 345 123, or text 4029) offers free, confidential help from financial mentors.

Where can I get ADHD support in New Zealand?

Start with your GP. Health New Zealand has information on ADHD in adults, and ADHD New Zealand lists support groups and resources. If you're struggling emotionally, free call or text 1737 any time to talk to a trained counsellor.

How do I get an ADHD assessment as an adult in NZ?

Start by talking to your GP about your symptoms and options, including a referral. Access to public adult assessment varies around the country, and many people use private psychiatrists or psychologists, which costs more. Health New Zealand's ADHD in adults page explains the process.
