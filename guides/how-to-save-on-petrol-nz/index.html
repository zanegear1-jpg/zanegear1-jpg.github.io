How to Save on Petrol in NZ: Pay Less per Litre, Use Fewer Litres and Cut Your Bills

Petrol prices in NZ hit record highs this spring. Here's how to pay less per litre, burn fewer litres, and claw back money from your other bills too.

Filling up has become painful. If you're searching for how to save on petrol in NZ, you're far from alone, and the good news is there's a lot you can do without changing your whole life. You can attack the cost from two sides: pay less for each litre, and use fewer of them. Then, while you're in money-saving mode, give your power, broadband, mobile and insurance bills the same treatment.

This guide covers the quick wins, the bigger commute decisions, and a once-a-year bill check, with a free checklist at the end.

Petrol prices NZ: why it hurts right now

A few numbers to put it in context:

  • Record highs. Gaspy data reported by CarExpert put the national average for 91 at about $3.53 a litre in early October 2026, up about 49c a litre in 28 days.
  • We're spending more and driving less. Westpac's Retail Spending Pulse for September 2026 found fuel spending up 28% on a year earlier, even though fuel volumes fell about 9%.
  • It's hitting budgets. In Genesis's June 2026 Smarter Energy Survey, 76% of New Zealanders said rising fuel costs were having a major or moderate impact on their household budget.

Pump prices move every day, so check a price app for today's numbers rather than relying on anything printed here.

Work out what petrol really costs you

Before you change anything, get your own number. It takes two minutes:

Weekly fuel cost = km per week × L/100km ÷ 100 × price per litre

For example, 250 km a week in a car that uses 8 L/100km is 20 litres. At $3.50 a litre that's $70 a week, or about $3,640 a year. Knock 10c a litre off and use 10% fewer litres, and you save around $460 a year. Small changes stack.

Fuel discounts NZ: pay less per litre

  • Use a price app. Gaspy is a free, crowd-sourced app showing prices near you. The gap between stations in the same suburb can be big, so a quick check before you fill pays off.
  • Use the dockets you already get. Supermarket fuel dockets and rewards programmes can take cents off each litre. Club+ lists 8c/L off at Z and Caltex in its current terms, and Everyday Rewards offers fuel discounts too. Read the expiry dates, litre caps and minimum spends, and don't buy groceries you don't need just to earn a docket.
  • Stack where the rules allow. Some programmes can be combined with a station's own app deal. Check the terms first.
  • Fuel cards. If you run a vehicle for work, a fuel card can bring a set discount and simpler records. Compare fees against the discount.

AA Smartfuel has gone

Lots of older advice still recommends AA Smartfuel, but it ended on 31 January 2024. Don't go hunting for it.

Use fewer litres: trips, habits, tyres

  • Combine errands into one loop, and do the furthest stop first so the engine is warm for the short hops.
  • Drive smoothly. Gentle acceleration, looking ahead and easing off early all save fuel. So does sticking to the speed limit on the open road.
  • Take the weight and drag off. Empty the boot and take roof racks off when you're not using them.
  • Check tyre pressures monthly against the sticker in the driver's door. Under-inflated tyres waste fuel and wear faster.
  • Keep up servicing. A car that's due a service won't be running at its best.

Is an e-bike worth it in NZ? Bus, carpool and EV maths

If you commute every day, the commute is where the big savings are. Compare honestly:

  • Bus or train. Use your regional transport card fare, check fare caps and concessions, and remember parking savings. Westpac's data shows public transport spending up about 12% since the end of February, so plenty of Kiwis are already trying it.
  • Carpool. Sharing two or three days a week with a colleague can halve that part of your fuel bill.
  • E-bike. Charging costs very little, so most of the cost is upfront. Payback period = upfront cost ÷ yearly saving. A $3,000 setup that saves $2,000 a year in petrol and parking pays for itself in about 18 months. One that saves $300 a year takes 10 years.
  • EV or hybrid. Lower running costs, but include road user charges. NZTA lists $76 per 1,000 km for light EVs and $38 for plug-in hybrids. Changing cars to save fuel only works if the numbers add up after the extra purchase cost.

Switch power company NZ: the once-a-year bill check

Petrol is the bill you see every week. But many households are also paying a "loyalty tax" on bills they never look at. Once a year:

  1. Power: put your last 12 months of usage into Powerswitch, Consumer NZ's free comparison tool. Check exit fees, then call your retailer or switch.
  2. Broadband: check whether your promo price has ended and what speed you really need.
  3. Mobile: look at how much data you actually use. Many people pay for far more than they need.
  4. Insurance: get quotes at renewal, comparing cover, excess and exclusions rather than just price.

When you ring, be polite, know your numbers, and ask for the retention or loyalty team if the first person can't help.

Free checklist: beat the pump this month

Make the savings stick

The trick is to turn this into a habit: one check before you fill up, one combined trip a week, one bill a month. Track what you spend for a month and you'll see exactly where the money goes.

Frequently asked questions

What is the cheapest way to save on petrol in NZ?

The free wins come first: check a price app before you fill up, use any supermarket or rewards discount you already earn, combine errands into one trip, keep your tyres at the pressure on the door sticker, and drive smoothly. None of these cost anything up front.

Is AA Smartfuel still available?

No. AA Smartfuel ended on 31 January 2024. Look at current options instead, such as supermarket fuel dockets, Club+ and Everyday Rewards, and check each programme's terms because the details change.

Is an e-bike worth it in NZ?

It can be if you have a regular commute of a few kilometres, somewhere safe to park it, and you'd otherwise be paying for petrol and parking. Divide the upfront cost (bike, lock, helmet, lights) by what you'd save each year to get a rough payback period, and be honest about hills, weather and how often you'd really ride.

Do EVs pay road user charges in NZ?

Yes. NZTA lists road user charges for light electric vehicles at $76 per 1,000 km and light plug-in hybrids at $38 per 1,000 km. Include that when you compare running costs, and check NZTA for current rates.

How do I switch power company in NZ?

Grab your last 12 months of usage from your bill or online account, run it through Powerswitch (a free Consumer NZ comparison tool), check for exit fees on your current plan, then either ring your current retailer to ask them to match or sign up with the new one. The new retailer usually handles the switch.
