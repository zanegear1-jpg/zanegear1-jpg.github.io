How to Make a Sourdough Starter in NZ (Plain Flour, 7 Days)

All you need is flour, water, a jar and a week. Here's a simple method that works in real Kiwi kitchens, cold winters and humid summers included.

Updated 8 October 2026 · 5 min read · Free guide

Sourdough has a reputation for being complicated, but a starter is just flour and water given time. If you've been wondering how to make a sourdough starter in NZ using ordinary supermarket flour, this guide gives you a simple seven-day method, notes for cold winter kitchens and humid summers, and a free checklist to track your feeds.

What you need

  • A clean jar (about 1 litre, glass or food-safe plastic) with a loose lid or cloth cover
  • Kitchen scales (helpful, not essential)
  • Flour: standard high-grade, with a little wholemeal or rye if you have it
  • Lukewarm water: tap water is usually fine
  • A spoon or spatula and a rubber band to mark the level

Sourdough starter recipe NZ: the 7-day method

This is a typical pattern. Your timing will vary with your kitchen's temperature.

  1. Day 1: Mix about 50 g flour + 50 g lukewarm water into a thick paste. Cover loosely and leave at room temperature.
  2. Days 2 to 7: Once a day, discard most of it (keep about 50 g), then feed 50 g flour + 50 g water. Stir well and scrape down the sides.
  3. Watch for life: bubbles, a rise and fall, and a smell that's yeasty, tangy or a bit like yoghurt. A harsh, rotten smell or coloured mould means start again.
  4. Ready test: when it reliably doubles within about 4 to 8 hours of a feed, it's ready to bake with.

Use a rubber band

Slip a rubber band around the jar at the level right after feeding. You'll see at a glance how much it has risen, and when it has peaked.

How to make sourdough starter in winter (and summer) in NZ

Cold winter kitchens slow everything down. Many NZ homes sit well below "room temperature" overnight. If your starter is sluggish:

  • Move it to a slightly warmer spot, such as near (not on) a heat pump outlet or in a hot water cupboard.
  • Use lukewarm water at each feed.
  • Be patient. It may take 10 to 14 days instead of seven.

Humid summers speed things up and raise the mould risk. Feed on time, keep the jar clean around the rim, and watch for any coloured fuzz.

Troubleshooting

Problem Try this
No bubbles after 4–5 days Warmer spot, consistent feeds, a spoon of wholemeal or rye
Grey liquid on top It's hungry: stir it in or pour it off, then feed
Rises then collapses fast It's peaking early: use it sooner or feed more flour
Fuzzy or coloured mould Discard, sterilise the jar and restart
Smells like nail polish Very hungry: feed more often for a couple of days

Your first bake (and a backup plan)

Once your starter is active, follow a trusted loaf recipe and keep notes. Flour brands and hydration vary, so your notebook will soon be more useful than any recipe.

If the starter is sulking and you need bread today, a simple yeast loaf is a great backup: roughly 500 g flour, 7 g dried yeast, 10 g salt and 300 to 320 ml water. Knead, prove until doubled, shape, prove again, then bake hot. Ovens vary, so start around 220°C and check your oven's guide. A hollow tap on the base usually means it's done.

Food safety: the non-negotiables

MPI's food safety advice is worth a read for any from-scratch cooking:

  • Wash your hands and keep benches and gear clean.
  • Keep raw and ready-to-eat foods separate.
  • Set your fridge between 2°C and 5°C and check it with a thermometer.
  • When in doubt, throw it out.

Allergies and intolerances

Sourdough is still made from wheat flour and contains gluten. If you have coeliac disease or an allergy, talk to a health professional before experimenting.

Keep the habit going

A four-week practice plan works well: start your starter (and try homemade butter) in week one, bake a first sandwich loaf in week two, make stock and a dip in week three, and do pizza or flatbreads on Friday of week four. Frequently asked questions

What flour should I use for a sourdough starter in NZ?

Standard high-grade flour from the supermarket works well. Adding a little wholemeal or rye flour in the first few days can help get things going, because it carries more natural yeasts and nutrients.

Why isn't my starter bubbling?

It's usually too cold. Kiwi kitchens can be chilly, especially in winter. Move the jar somewhere slightly warmer (never on a hot element), keep feeding consistently, and give it a few more days.

Is it safe if my starter grows mould?

No. If you see fuzzy or coloured mould (pink, orange, green or black), throw it out, wash and sterilise the jar, and start again. A clear or greyish liquid layer on top (often called hooch) is different and just means it's hungry.

How do I store my starter between bakes?

If you bake once a week or less, keep it in the fridge and feed it weekly. Take it out the day before you bake and give it a feed or two at room temperature until it's bubbly and rising again.

How long does it take to make a sourdough starter?

Usually about seven days in a warm kitchen, but 10 to 14 days is common in cold NZ winter kitchens. It's ready when it reliably doubles within about 4 to 8 hours of a feed.
